01

Simple Tenses

Present · Past · Future  |  Active & Passive Voice

Simple tenses express actions as complete facts — habits, routines, completed events, and future predictions. No emphasis on duration or connection between events. The most common tenses in everyday English.

Present Simple

Habits · Facts · Routines
Past NOW ● Future
ActiveSubject + base verb (he/she/it → verb+s)
She works · They play · He teaches
Passiveam / is / are + past participle
It is made · They are sold · He is taught
Uses
habitsroutinesfactsschedulesinstructions
🕐 Time words

always · usually · often · sometimes · rarely · never · every day · on Mondays · in general

Examples
The chef cooks the meal every evening.
Water boils at 100°C. (fact)
The meal is cooked by the chef.
English is spoken worldwide.
i
In passive, the agent (by + person) is often dropped when it's obvious or unimportant: "The windows are cleaned every week."
✕
Common error: "She work here." → "She works here." Never use present simple for actions happening right now — use present continuous instead.

Past Simple

Completed actions · Past states
Action ● Now Future
ActiveSubject + verb2 (past form / irregular)
I worked · She went · They said
Passivewas / were + past participle
It was built · They were told
Uses
completed actionspast statessequences
🕐 Time words

yesterday · last week · ago · in 2020 · on Monday · when · then · at 6 o'clock · after that

Examples
The students completed the task yesterday.
Shakespeare wrote many plays in the 16th century.
The task was completed on time.
Many plays were written by Shakespeare.
i
Irregular verbs: go→went, buy→bought, write→wrote, take→took. The 3rd form (e.g. written) is used for passive.
✕
Common error: "I have seen him yesterday." → Use past simple with specific past time: "I saw him yesterday."

Future Simple

Predictions · Decisions · Promises
Past Now Future ●
Activewill + base verb (all subjects)
I will go · She will arrive · They will win
Passivewill be + past participle
It will be done · They will be told
Uses
predictionsdecisionspromisesoffers
🕐 Time words

tomorrow · next week/year · soon · later · in the future · one day · in 2030

Examples
They will announce the winner tonight.
I'll help you with that. (spontaneous offer)
The winner will be announced tonight.
Results will be published soon.
i
Will vs Going to: "I will call you" = decided right now. "I'm going to call you" = already planned before this moment.
✕
Common error: "When she will arrive..." → Never use 'will' in time clauses: "When she arrives..."
02

Continuous Tenses

Present · Past · Future  |  Active & Passive Voice

Continuous (progressive) tenses emphasise that an action is in progress at a specific moment. Always formed with: be + verb-ing. Stative verbs (know, want, love, believe, understand) are not used in continuous tenses.

Present Continuous

Happening now · Temporary
Past ▶ NOW Future
Activeam / is / are + verb-ing
I am working · She is studying
Passiveam / is / are + being + past participle
It is being repaired · They are being tested
Uses
happening nowtemporaryfuture planschanging trends
🕐 Time words

now · at the moment · currently · right now · today · this week · still · Look! · Listen!

Examples
She is writing an important report right now.
I'm meeting the director tomorrow. (arrangement)
A new bridge is being built near the city.
The road is being repaired this week.
i
Spelling: drop final -e (make→making), double consonant after short vowel (run→running), -ie → -ying (lie→lying).
✕
Error: "I am knowing the answer." → Stative verb! Say: "I know the answer."

Past Continuous

Background · Interrupted action
▶ Ongoing Now Future
Activewas / were + verb-ing
I was sleeping · They were talking
Passivewas / were + being + past participle
The road was being repaired
Uses
interrupted actionbackground sceneparallel past
🕐 Time words

while · when · as · at 8 p.m. yesterday · this time last week · all morning · the whole evening

Examples
I was watching TV when the phone rang.
While she was cooking, he was setting the table.
The museum was being renovated when we visited.
Meals were being served all evening.
i
Key pattern: was/were -ing (long background) + past simple (short interruption).
✕
Error: "She was knowing the answer." → Stative verb! Say: "She knew the answer."

Future Continuous

Ongoing at a future moment
Now Future pt ▶ Ongoing
Active onlywill be + verb-ing
I will be working · She will be travelling
⚠ No passive form exists
Uses
ongoing at future momentpolite enquiriesfuture routine
🕐 Time words

this time tomorrow · at 6 p.m. next Friday · next week at this time

Examples
This time tomorrow, I will be flying to London.
Will you be using the car this evening? (polite)
She will be teaching all day on Wednesday.
i
Use for polite questions about plans: "Will you be needing the report?" — less direct than "Will you need it?"
✕
No passive: "It will be being built" is ungrammatical. Restructure the sentence entirely.
03

Perfect Tenses

Present · Past · Future  |  Active & Passive Voice

Perfect tenses connect two time points — they show how a past action relates to the present, or how one past action preceded another. Always formed with: have / had / will have + past participle.

Present Perfect

Past action → present result
✦ Action Result NOW Future
Activehas / have + past participle
I have finished · She has arrived
Passivehas / have + been + past participle
It has been completed · They have been invited
Uses
past→present linklife experiencerecent newsunfinished time
🕐 Time words

already · yet · just · ever · never · recently · lately · since · for · today · this week · so far

Examples
I have lost my keys. (result: I can't get in)
Have you ever been to Japan? (life experience)
The report has been sent to the director.
The project has been completed ahead of schedule.
i
Already / Yet: "I've already eaten." (positive) vs "Have you eaten yet?" / "I haven't finished yet." (negative/question)
✕
Error: "I have seen him yesterday." Never use present perfect with a specific past time. Say: "I saw him yesterday."

Past Perfect

Earlier of two past events
✦ Earlier Later past Now
Activehad + past participle (all subjects)
I had finished · She had left
Passivehad + been + past participle
It had been repaired · They had been warned
Uses
earlier pastreported speech3rd conditionalnarrative
🕐 Time words

before · after · when · by the time · already · just · never · once · until

Examples
When I arrived, she had already left.
He said he had never seen anything like it.
By the time we got there, the decision had been made.
The letter had been sent the day before.
i
Only use past perfect when sequence would otherwise be unclear. It is the "past of the past." If 'before'/'after' already clarifies order, past simple for both is also acceptable.
✕
Tip: Don't overuse it. "She left before he arrived" is perfectly clear without past perfect.

Future Perfect

Completed before a future deadline
Now ✦ Done by Deadline
Activewill have + past participle
I will have finished · She will have left
Passivewill have been + past participle
It will have been completed
Uses
completed before futurefuture deadlineformal/academic
🕐 Time words

by tomorrow · by next week · by the time + clause · before she leaves · by 2030

Examples
By Friday, they will have finished the report.
I will have lived here for 10 years by next June.
The new school will have been built by 2026.
The work will have been completed by then.
i
"By" is the key trigger word. "By the time + clause" or "by + time expression" almost always signals this tense.
✕
Note: Less common in spoken English. More typical in formal and academic writing and B2+ exam tasks.
04

Perfect Continuous Tenses

Present · Past · Future  |  ACTIVE ONLY — no passive forms

Perfect continuous tenses emphasise the duration of an ongoing activity up to a reference point. They combine the "perfect" idea (linking two time points) with the "continuous" idea (ongoing action).
⚠ KEY EXAM POINT: No passive forms exist for any Perfect Continuous tense. Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous are ACTIVE ONLY.

Present Perfect Continuous

Duration from past until now
■ Started ▶ Still NOW Future
Active onlyhas / have + been + verb-ing
I have been working · She has been studying
⚠ No passive form
Uses
duration until nowrecent activitytemporary ongoing
🕐 Time words

since (start point) · for (period) · all day · all week · lately · recently · how long

Examples
She has been studying for five hours. (still studying)
I've been waiting since 9 o'clock!
Your eyes are red — have you been crying?
They have been living here for twenty years.
i
Since vs For: "since Monday" (starting point) vs "for three days" (duration). Both mark how long the action has continued.
✕
Error: "I have been knowing him." → Stative verb! Say: "I have known him for years." (present perfect simple)

Past Perfect Continuous

Duration before a past event
■ Started ▶ Past point Now
Active onlyhad + been + verb-ing
I had been waiting · She had been working
⚠ No passive form
Uses
duration before pastcause of past situation
🕐 Time words

for · since · before · when · by the time · all day (up to past point)

Examples
She was exhausted because she had been working all night.
They had been waiting for 2 hours when the bus finally arrived.
He had been learning English for years before he moved abroad.
i
This tense answers "Why?" in the past. Result = past simple; cause/duration = past perfect continuous: "She was tired because she had been running."
✕
Note: Don't use for stative verbs. "She had known him for years" (NOT "had been knowing").

Future Perfect Continuous

Duration up to a future deadline
Now ▶ Ongoing ■ Future pt
Active onlywill have been + verb-ing
I will have been working · She will have been studying
⚠ No passive form
Uses
duration up to futureemphasis on length
🕐 Time words

by next month · by 2030 · by the time + clause · for (period)

Examples
By next June, she will have been teaching for 30 years.
When he retires, he will have been working here for 40 years.
By the time you arrive, I will have been cooking for 2 hours.
i
Use when both the future deadline AND the length of the activity need to be expressed. The least common of all 12 tenses.
✕
Note: Rarely used in informal speech. Future perfect simple is usually preferred in everyday conversation.
05

Conditionals

1st · 2nd · 3rd  |  Active & Passive Voice

Conditionals express hypothetical situations and their consequences. Two clauses: the if-clause (condition) + the main clause (result). Clauses can swap order. The if-clause sets up the scenario; the main clause shows what follows.

First Conditional

Real future possibility
✓ Possible & Likely
If (now) → Result Future
ActiveIf + present simple, will + base verb
If it rains, we will cancel the trip.
PassiveIf + pres. simple, will be + past participle
If the form is received, it will be processed.
Uses
real possibilitywarningspromisesinstructions
🔑 Key words

if · unless · when · as soon as · as long as · provided that · in case

Examples
If they hurry, they will catch the bus.
Unless you study harder, you won't pass the exam.
If the application is submitted, it will be reviewed within 5 days.
If the law is passed, it will be enforced immediately.
i
Unless = "if not": "Unless it rains" = "If it doesn't rain." The if-clause always uses present simple, even though the meaning is future.
✕
Error: "If she will arrive early, we will start." → No 'will' in the if-clause! → "If she arrives early, we will start."

Second Conditional

Unreal / imaginary present
✗ Unlikely / Imaginary
Imaginary → Unreal result
ActiveIf + past simple, would + base verb
If I had more time, I would learn piano.
PassiveIf + past simple, would be + past participle
If the law were changed, it would be accepted.
Uses
unreal presentunlikely scenarioadvicepolite requests
🔑 Key words

if only · suppose · what if · imagine · were to

Examples
If I were rich, I would travel around the world.
If I were you, I would apply for that job. (advice)
If the policy were reviewed, it would be changed immediately.
If the rule were revised, it would be applied consistently.
i
"If I were you" is a fixed advice expression — always use were, never was. The past simple doesn't mean past time — it signals distance from reality.
✕
Error: "If I would have more money..." → Never 'would' in the if-clause! → "If I had more money, I would buy a car."

Third Conditional

Unreal past / regrets
✗✗ Impossible (past)
Unreal past Imagined result Now
ActiveIf + past perfect, would have + past participle
If she had studied, she would have passed.
PassiveIf + past perfect, would have been + past participle
If the check had been done, it would have been caught.
Uses
regretspast criticismimpossible changespeculation
🔑 Key words

if only · had (done) · might have · could have · would have

Examples
If she had studied harder, she would have passed the exam.
I wouldn't have been late if the traffic had been lighter.
If the safety check had been completed, the accident would have been avoided.
If the letter had been sent, it would have been received in time.
i
Mixed conditional: 3rd if-clause + 2nd main clause = past condition, present result: "If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now."
✕
Error: "If I would have known..." → Never 'would have' in the if-clause! → "If I had known, I would have helped."
TypeIf-clauseMain clauseRealityExample
1st present simple will + base verb Possible ✓ "If it rains, I will stay home."
2nd past simple would + base verb Unlikely ✗ "If I were rich, I would travel."
3rd past perfect would have + p.p. Impossible ✗✗ "If I had known, I would have helped."
⭐
Golden rule for all conditionals: The word "will" or "would" NEVER goes inside the if-clause in standard conditionals. This applies to all three types without exception.
